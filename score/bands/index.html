Band guide

Back to scoring

Bands are written from bat's left to right and elbow to wrist

Band letters: p c e w x. Every silver band is p.

Left wing and right wing separated by "-". e.g., pc-x means porzana band and colored band on left wing (porzana closer to elbow, color closer to wrist), and no bands on right wing.

Bands are written left-right (the bat's left and right). This means that if the bat is facing you, it has the same left and right as you, and if it is facing away from you, it has the opposite.

In the feeder clips you look down on the bat's back: with its head toward the top of the screen, its left wing is on your left.

The bands

p silver (porzana)

Classic silver lipped, dull under IR light. Older notes split silver bands into p and a second letter; they are all one type now. Every silver band is p.

On camera: A grey ring with a raised lip. The most common band.

c colored

Colored bird bands that close into a ring, fitted by cutting slit in bat wing and passing ring thru. Completely smooth (no numbers). Teeth's old purple ring also counts as c.

On camera: The camera records in infrared, so the colour does not show; a c looks like a smooth, plain ring.

e envelope

p bands with smaller bands wrapped around them, resulting in a very flat non-lip portion of the ring, can look triangular at certain angles.

On camera: From some angles an e looks like a p. The lab is moving away from e bands.

w white

White painted stripe around center of p-band.

On camera: The brightest band on camera: a white ring.

x none

No band on that wing.

On camera: A bare wrist.
